## 1. Substantive issues (affect the main conclusion)

### 1.1 The opportunity baseline is the nominated-alter pool, which is endogenous to the outcome

- The offset is `log(p/(1-p))`, where `p` is each wave's share of the ego's group among nominated student alters with known religion.
- That pool is built from the same ties being modeled, so in-group choices feed back into the baseline they are meant to correct.
- Composition differs: Catholics are about 80% of nominated alters (wave average 0.797) but 73% of egos (525/718 with religion known).
- Sensitivity (Model 1, measured on the pre-fix sample, 2026-10-22): replacing the alter-pool offset with ego-cohort composition changes the coefficients as follows.

| Coefficient | Alter-pool offset (paper) | Cohort-composition offset |
|---|---|---|
| Catholic intercept | 0.267 | 0.630 |
| No Religion | 0.549 | −0.187 |
| Other Religion | 1.718 | 0.772 |
| Protestant | 0.497 | 0.007 |

- Only Other Religion keeps a positive excess under the cohort baseline. The abstract's claim that non-Catholics collectively exceed the Catholic majority, and the MDH support that rests on it, depend on the baseline choice.
- Neither baseline is the true opportunity structure. The student-body composition is not in the data. The manuscript's statement that Catholics are "nearly three-quarters of the student body" describes the cohort, not a measured student-body quantity.
- **Needed:** a decision on the baseline, plus a sensitivity analysis reported alongside Table 1. Not yet decided.

### 1.2 Excess-probability comparisons are not comparable across group sizes

- `10_bootstrap_sensitivity.R` header and Table 3 notes say excess rates are "directly comparable across groups of different sizes." This is incorrect.
- If all groups share an in-group odds ratio `h`, the excess probability is `p(1-p)(h-1)/(hp+1-p)`, which depends on the group's baseline `p`. For example, at `h = 3`, the excess is about 0.123 for a group with `p = 0.8` and about 0.139 for `p = 0.09`, with identical preferences.
- Table 3 Panels A–B therefore mix baseline with preference.
- On a logit scale, a constant odds ratio gives a constant coefficient (`log h`). Panels C–D are the better basis for group comparisons.
- Reviewer 2's undirected-network argument applies to all of these measures. No script addresses it. The logit-offset model does not model reciprocity or the symmetry constraint.

### 1.3 The alter-popularity control partly contains the outcome tie

- About 52% of analytic ties go to an alter whose in-degree in that wave is 1, so the focal nomination is the only one counted. Per-wave shares of alters with in-degree 1 are 85–91%.
- Leave-focal-tie-out sensitivity (Model 3, pre-fix sample): group coefficients are unchanged (No Religion 0.549, Other Religion 1.717, Protestant 0.496). Alter popularity changes from −0.191 (p = 0.13) to −0.132 (p = 0.105).
- The Reviewer 1 conclusion (group effects are not a degree artifact) survives. The Table 1 alter-popularity coefficient should not be interpreted as it stands.
- **Needed:** switch `alter_pop` to the leave-focal-tie-out version (`alter_popularity - 1`, then `log1p`) in `02_dyadic_models_pooled.R` and `02_dyadic_models_interaction.R`, rerun, and update the text.

### 1.4 Yule's Q claim is wrong in the Results text

- Results (around line 106) say the expected value of Q under random mixing is 0 "regardless of group size."
- Table 2's null means for Other Religion range from −0.10 to −0.37 across waves (−0.37 in W1, −0.10 in W5). That contradicts the sentence. The next paragraph of the manuscript acknowledges the bias, so the two passages conflict.
- **Needed:** rewrite the sentence (not yet done).

## 4. Not addressed by the current design

- **Alter-level dependence:** the same alter can be nominated by several egos, but standard errors cluster only by ego. Two-way clustering by ego and alter has not been tried.
- **Reviewer 2 identification:** the data cannot separate preference from structural constraint. Multiple networks or a reciprocity model would be needed.
- **Power:** Other Religion has 19 egos in the regression sample. Change over time for non-Catholic groups cannot be tested with useful precision.
- **Ordinal coding:** `close_num` must use `case_when` (Distant 1, LessThanClose 2, MerelyClose 3, EspeciallyClose 4). Do not reintroduce `as.numeric(as.factor(close))`.

## 5. What the analysis can and cannot support

**Supported, with caveats:**
- Methods descriptives (Section 2 of the manuscript) match the data.
- Each group's same-religion ties exceed its own nominated-pool baseline (Yule's Q, permutation test). Caveat: the baseline is endogenous (1.1).
- Catholic-vs-non-Catholic logit contrasts under the nominated-pool baseline. Caveat: 1.1.
- Group coefficients are robust to degree controls, including the leave-focal-tie-out version.
- No reliable ranking among Protestant, No Religion, and Other Religion. This conclusion is consistent across the bootstrap and Wald tests.

**Not supported by the current design:**
- Separating preference from structure (Reviewer 2).
- Establishing MDH over MEH independently of the baseline choice.
- Detecting change over time for groups with 19–46 egos.
- Precise estimates for Other Religion.

## 7. Pitfalls for future agents

- **Global environment:** `source(..., local = TRUE)` assigns into the global environment when called at top level. It keeps objects local only inside a function. Use `source(..., local = new.env())` and access objects explicitly.
- **here() caching:** the project root is fixed at first load. Verify where outputs were written before comparing reruns.
- **Overleaf:** fetch and diff before pushing (`git fetch overleaf && git diff HEAD overleaf/main`). Co-authors edit prose in the Overleaf web editor.
- **Sign conventions:** Wald Panel C/D log-odds differences are Group 1 minus Group 2 (after the fix).
- **Stale memory:** AGENTS.md Sections 0, 4, and 8 describe `local = TRUE` as safe and the git state as uncommitted. Both are out of date.
